### The two external failures are correct and deliberate

**EDMSA Scenario A CO2 2035: 90.8 Mt against their 124.** A real disagreement between two models
with different demand and build assumptions. It is doing its job as a flag.

**NTCSA MTSAO risk-adjusted 2030, OCGT utilisation 58.9% against about 45%.** The demand mapping
was derived rather than tuned - 264 TWh national over a ratio of 1.187 gives 222.4 TWh on this
model's basis, which is +18%, not the +15% the check used to carry. At that demand our peakers
work harder than NTCSA's, and the check says so. Tuning demand back to 15% to keep it green would
be fitting the input to the answer.

### How to invoke them - this still bites

Ten harnesses take the root as `argv[2]` and default to `.`. Two default to `testroot`. Three
ignore the argument entirely: `stress_suite.js` (hardcodes paths relative to the working
directory), `eng5.js` and `jsdom_local2.js` (both hardcode `testroot/index.html`, run from the
PARENT), and `validate_outputs.js` (hardcodes `path.resolve('testroot')`, also from the parent).

`profiles.json` must be at the REPO ROOT. Without it the suite produces eleven false failures
across five harnesses, and a run without it is not evidence.

### Four harnesses now wait on the page, not on a stopwatch

`validate_benchmarks`, `validate_consistency`, `validate_response` and `validate_outputs` poll
`window.GTZA_READY` instead of sleeping a fixed 4 to 4.5 seconds. The old delay remains as a
ceiling.

## Known distortions - flag when touching anything downstream

**Hydra Central is understated.** An unknown slice of the Northern Cape's fleet physically
connects at Hydra rather than in the province. Restoring it needs named operational projects
matched to substations, which is the same data gap as the missing private plant. Global Energy
Monitor's trackers are the next thing to try; OpenStreetMap gives geometry but not commissioning
dates.

**RMIPPPP is excluded from the regional file.** About 225 MW across three provinces with no
published split. `by_source.rmipppp` exists to hold it.

**The model answers bulk resource adequacy only.** Not distribution reliability, not resilience.
That is the right scope for load shedding and the wrong scope for why a Johannesburg suburb loses
power. Do not let a distributed-generation case be argued from these results.

**Demand is a TWh target, not a percentage.** The same growth setting lands on a different TWh in
every preset, because the base moves with the rooftop fleet: +5% is 183.3 TWh on Deep
decarbonisation and 173.0 on Fossil-free. Compare scenarios on TWh served, never on the slider.
