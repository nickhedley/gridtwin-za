Run the full model

network-aware MIP · HiGHS · runs in your browser
Re-solves the whole system as a true mixed-integer programming (MIP) model, where coal, storage and corridor flows are fully optimised across all 10 regions. Most worth running for high-renewables scenarios: the instant model commits coal in 8-hour blocks, so it follows a midday solar trough only approximately — benchmarking puts it within 1% of optimal for today's system, widening to around 6–8% once 25 GW or more of new solar is added.

Where To Build

nodal prototype · indicative

Pick a region and technology for new capacity — wind, solar, coal, gas CCGT, nuclear, or battery storage. If the local grid has spare headroom on today's real transmission network, it's free. If not, you'll see the actual grid-build charge needed to unlock it — a corridor-specific alternative to the flat national grid adder used above. All technologies — including CCGT and batteries — carry this charge when they exceed regional headroom, though their lower capacity factors (CCGT ~15%, battery ~15%) mean the R/MWh figure is higher than for coal or nuclear at equivalent corridor length.

Note on the map above: your allocated region gets a visible bump (radius + a small solar/wind dot). But because allocations feed the same national sliders the rest of the app uses, nodes like PE and EC will also keep growing with the national wind total regardless of where you actually sited it — the underlying dispatch engine is single-node and has no notion of "this MW is physically in region X". Only the dot and the node's own bump reflect your real siting choice.

On Run the full model (above the KPIs), the optimiser solves the real 10-region network for a full 8,760-hour year: real demand, transfer-limited corridors, plant siting (rooftop PV, CSP, Cahora Bassa imports, pumped storage), and every allocation made here. Coal carries real per-region ramp-rate limits, per-unit minimum up/down times and start-up costs. Storage dispatches on real network-wide price arbitrage, holding back capacity for its own region before exporting elsewhere. Grid-enhancing technologies, if enabled, target only congested corridors found via a baseline pass first. On success the KPIs, energy mix and hourly chart above switch to these results — only the load-shedding risk panel stays on the national engine.

Model assumptions & caveats

What this is: a simplified national-level digital twin. Hourly demand, wind and solar profiles are drawn from 2025 Eskom data (the most recent complete year available). Fleet parameters — EAF, OCGT load factor, rooftop PV, structural demand level — are calibrated to Eskom's weekly system status reports (June–July 2026): winter evening peaks ≈ 26–27.3 GW, EAF ≈ 64 %, OCGT load factor ≈ 1.3 %, 8.3 GW embedded rooftop PV, post-2023 structural demand decline. Dispatch is by merit order: rooftop PV nets off demand → wind / utility PV / CSP → nuclear, hydro, Cahora Bassa imports → coal (× EAF) → pumped storage & batteries → gas CCGT → diesel OCGT → unserved energy. One stage of load shedding ≈ 1 000 MW of unserved demand. Storage recharges from surplus renewables and off-peak coal headroom.

Outage risk: coal availability follows a mean-corrected daily AR(1) process around the EAF slider, with volatility calibrated to the multi-week swings in Eskom's reported unplanned outages. The risk panel dispatches 60 such synthetic years and reports load shedding as probabilities. Weather and demand noise are held fixed across runs, so the bands reflect outage risk only — real years also vary in weather.

What it is not: transmission is schematic only (no network constraints), no unit commitment or operating reserves. For decision-grade work, use PyPSA-RSA — this app is the intuition layer in front of it.
