Note on the cost figures: System cost and Avg energy cost count fuel, carbon and the capex of newly-built capacity only – existing plant costs are treated as sunk, so today's paid-off coal and nuclear look cheap. Replacement cost is the opposite lens: it prices every MWh (existing and new) at its full lifecycle cost, i.e. what this mix would cost if built entirely new at today's prices. This means it charges existing coal by its new-build cost and makes a coal-heavy grid look expensive. Neither is a tariff or the complete picture. Where To Build

nodal prototype · indicative

Pick a region and technology for new capacity — wind, solar, coal, gas CCGT, nuclear, or battery storage. If the local grid has spare headroom on today's real transmission network, it's free. If not, you'll see the actual grid-build charge needed to unlock it — a corridor-specific alternative to the flat national grid adder used above. CCGT and batteries never carry this charge (see the Nodal Simulation panel below for why).

Note on the map above: your allocated region gets a visible bump (radius + a small solar/wind dot). But because allocations feed the same national sliders the rest of the app uses, nodes like PE and EC will also keep growing with the national wind total regardless of where you actually sited it — the underlying dispatch engine is single-node and has no notion of "this MW is physically in region X". Only the dot and the node's own bump reflect your real siting choice.

Note on the Nodal Simulation

what it models · caveats

Runs the real 10-region network model for a full 8,760-hour year: real demand, transfer-limited corridors, and real plant siting (rooftop PV, CSP, Cahora Bassa imports, pumped storage) - plus new coal/CCGT/nuclear/battery siting via "Where To Build" above (coal and nuclear carry a real grid-build charge; CCGT and batteries don't, since neither sustains enough utilisation to justify one). Coal has a real per-region ramp-rate limit (ramp-up only for now - ramp-down isn't modelled yet). Storage dispatches on real network-wide price arbitrage, holding back some capacity for its own region's near-term needs before exporting elsewhere. Grid-enhancing technologies, if enabled, target only congested corridors found via a real baseline pass first. On success, the KPIs, energy mix, hourly chart, and map above all switch to these real results - only the load-shedding risk panel stays on the national engine. Use the "Run full year" button on the map above.

Model assumptions & caveats

What this is: a simplified national-level digital twin. One year (8 760 h) of synthetic demand, wind and solar profiles — calibrated to Eskom's weekly system status reports (June–July 2026): winter evening peaks ≈ 26–27.3 GW, fleet EAF ≈ 64 %, OCGT load factor ≈ 1.3 %, 8.3 GW of embedded rooftop PV, and the post-2023 structural demand decline. Dispatch is by merit order: rooftop PV nets off demand → wind / utility PV / CSP → nuclear, hydro, Cahora Bassa imports → coal (× EAF) → pumped storage & batteries → gas CCGT → diesel OCGT → unserved energy. One stage of load shedding ≈ 1 000 MW of unserved demand. Storage recharges from surplus renewables and off-peak coal headroom.

Outage risk: coal availability follows a mean-corrected daily AR(1) process around the EAF slider, with volatility calibrated to the multi-week swings in Eskom's reported unplanned outages. The risk panel dispatches 60 such synthetic years and reports load shedding as probabilities. Weather and demand noise are held fixed across runs, so the bands reflect outage risk only — real years also vary in weather.

What it is not: transmission is schematic only (no network constraints), no unit commitment or operating reserves. For decision-grade work, use PyPSA-RSA — this app is the intuition layer in front of it.

Built as a demonstrator in the spirit of PyPSA-CA. Fleet capacities and profiles approximate the 2026 South African system (calibrated to Eskom weekly system status reports, June–July 2026). All outputs are indicative — this is an educational scenario explorer, not planning or investment advice.
