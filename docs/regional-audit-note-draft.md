## Draft text

The network's binding limit is the Karoo's exits. On today's corridors the two circuits out of the Northern Cape toward the North West and
the Free State sit at their limit 8,700 hours a year in 2035 and 2040, and the Cape and Eastern Cape exits behind them; the least-cost build
curtails 57 TWh in 2035 and 106 TWh in 2040 for want of them [EC]. An independent cross-check broadly agrees on the binding corridors.

Today's fleet is unconstrained: the 2025 and 2026 fleets curtail nothing on the same table, and no corridor binds for a thousand hours [EC].

The order of the lines matters more than their number at today's build rate. At 293 km a year, the plan's inter-area lines by their own dates
leave 2032 curtailing 20 TWh; the same kilometres in the order of the binding corridors leave 9 TWh, R7.6bn a year apart [EC, earlier table].

For the network, lines beat batteries. Two gigawatts of lithium at the exits cuts 2035's curtailment by one terawatt-hour for R2.8bn a year;
the same money in corridors cuts it by 45 TWh, and the corridors are a net saving at either value of unserved energy where the lithium is a
net cost at the engine's [EC, earlier table].

Gauteng's 2040 shortfall is a Karoo-exit problem. With the plan's lines, 58 GWh is unserved in Gauteng; the cheapest store the model chooses
sits at the Eastern Cape and Hydra Central exits, not in Gauteng, and clears half of it; 20 GW of corridor for R5.8bn a year clears all of it
and nets R55bn at R12,300 per MWh [EC].

Caveat: a zonal, fixed-build test. Ten areas with no limits inside them; the pathway's build held fixed; one weather year; perfect foresight;
thermal and loadability ratings with no voltage or stability limit. It finds where the corridors bind and what they are worth, not what to build.
